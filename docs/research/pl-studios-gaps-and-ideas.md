# Explain the match, don't just score it

Premier League Studios is a new, software-first production hub in Olympia, London. It took over the League's international output from IMG in 2026/27 and produces all 380 matches and about 6,000 hours a season for 55 broadcast partners in 189 markets. Its stated goal is creative control and a direct-to-fan future, not new on-screen intelligence. The PL ecosystem around it, Microsoft's Companion apps included, already offers on-demand chat, FPL advice, win probability and tracking-stat overlays. What it lacks is the layer rival leagues have shipped and fans keep asking for: named, explainable live metrics, an automatic story feed for producers, catch-up for fans who join late, and multilingual explanations timed for half-time and other breaks. That gap is the hackathon opportunity. The team should not pitch a chatbot, a win-probability widget, an FPL points predictor or plain translation, because the PL and Microsoft already have or have announced each of these. The strongest entry is an evidence-grounded "explanation engine": synthetic events become typed insights (control vs chaos, pressure swings, rhythm), every generated sentence is checked against the numbers, and the output is timed overlay JSON plus persona- and language-specific narration that rolls up into half-time and full-time briefs. Match-Room already sits at the half-time end of that pipeline, so it is the natural base to extend.

*Labelling convention: sentences with an inline link are sourced facts. Sentences marked **Inference** are the analyst's judgement, drawn from the sources but not stated in them. Design proposals for the team are marked **Proposal**.*

## Studios inherited a 380-match machine and is rebuilding it for control and direct-to-fan

The clubs voted unanimously on 22 Nov 2024 to bring all international media production and distribution in-house from 2026/27. This ended about 20 years of IMG running Premier League Productions (PLP) from Stockley Park ([Premier League/IMG](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4172032)). The new base is **73,000 sq ft at One Olympia, Kensington, on a 20-year commitment** ([SVG Europe](https://www.svgeurope.org/blog/headlines/premier-league-confirms-it-is-set-to-establish-its-new-international-broadcast-production-hq-at-olympia-in-central-london/)). Qvest is the systems integrator for a "mainly software-based environment, leveraging cloud technology where it makes sense", built to produce all 380 matches ([SVG Europe, IBC 2025](https://www.svgeurope.org/blog/news-roundup/ibc-2025-qvest-building-a-true-premier-league-facility/)). Technology is led by Andy Beale, formerly BT Sport's chief engineer ([SVG Europe](https://www.svgeurope.org/blog/headlines/the-premier-league-snaps-up-andy-beale-as-director-of-technology-to-bolster-broadcast-ambitions/)). Beale led a 2021 cloud remote-production proof of concept on a PL match that used up to 70% less on-site equipment ([SportsPro](https://www.sportspro.com/news/premier-league-trial-sustainability-cloud-based-broadcasting-net-zero/)). The planned output is **6,000+ hours a season to 55 partners in 189 markets** ([NAB 2026](https://nab26.mapyourshow.com/8_0/sessions/speaker-details.cfm?speakerid=423)).

Studios inherits PLP's slate. That covers live international coverage of every match, a 24/7 channel, wraparound and magazine shows (Premier League Live, Fantasy Premier League Show, Generation xG, Match Officials Mic'd Up), and special feeds such as Goal Rush and Data Zone ([Wikipedia: PLP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League_Productions)). In the PLP era, the operation also ran a Matchday Social Channel that produced 20–30 videos a day for broadcasters in real time ([Broadcast](https://broadcastnow.co.uk/production/img-scores-premier-league-productions-renewal/5170495.article)). Studios also produces the channel for **Premier League+**, the League's first direct-to-consumer service, which launched in Singapore with StarHub in August 2026. Premier League+ offers multi-camera angles and "live data overlays" ([SportsPro](https://www.sportspro.com/news/broadcast-ott/premier-league-plus-dtc-streaming-singapore-starhub-may-2026/)). **Inference:** Studios now serves three customers from one content engine: international rights holders taking B2B feeds, the League's own DTC product, and the PL app and website.

Live production is a hub model. Host broadcasters such as Sky, working with OB providers like NEP, shoot the domestic match, and feeds travel over fibre to the central hub ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250908538717/en/NEP-Europes-New-OB-Facilities-Combine-Innovation-and-Comfort-for-Premier-League-Coverage); [SVG Europe 2014](https://www.svgeurope.org/blog/headlines/svgeimg-tour-conference-nick-moody-on-imgs-giant-premier-league-productions-operation/)). Historically, graphics ran on deltatre's Magma templates filled from automated Opta feeds ([Live-Production.tv](https://www.live-production.tv/news/products/deltatre-scores-premier-league-productions-graphics-contract)). Tracking-based overlays came from Genius Sports / Second Spectrum through Data Zone, which shows passing accuracy, shot speed, sprints and pitch maps in an L-bar for one featured match per round ([SVG](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2023/10/05/premier-league-productions-partners-with-genius-sports-to-deliver-premier-league-data-zone/)). **No 2026/27 graphics, playout or cloud vendor list for Studios has been published.** Headcount, gallery count and the name of the Studios managing director are also not public. The current overlay pipeline is therefore unknown, and the team should describe its output as a data contract that any graphics engine can consume rather than claim to replace a specific system.

The data underneath is tightly licensed. Football DataCo licenses official event data exclusively to Stats Perform (Opta), which since 2025/26 is also "official insights provider" through OptaAI ([Stats Perform](https://www.statsperform.com/press/expanded-partnership-with-football-dataco/)). Tracking and betting data go to Genius Sports until 2029 ([Sportcal](https://www.sportcal.com/betting/genius-and-fdc-extend-betting-data-rights-deal-to-2029/)). **Inference:** this is why the hackathon requires synthetic data. It is also why a feed that mimics the shape of a real one and can be swapped for the official feed later is a credible selling point rather than a workaround.

Executives describe Studios in terms of control and optionality. Paul Molnar says it gives "full creative and operational control" and faster response to platform needs ([TVBEurope](https://www.tvbeurope.com/live-production/qvest-to-design-and-implement-new-production-facility-for-english-premier-league); [SVG Europe](https://www.svgeurope.org/blog/news-roundup/ibc-2025-qvest-building-a-true-premier-league-facility/)). Richard Masters says "for the first time, the Premier League is going to have its own customers" ([SportsPro](https://www.sportspro.com/news/broadcast-ott/premier-league-plus-dtc-streaming-singapore-starhub-may-2026/)). The commercial stakes explain the emphasis: **international media revenue (£1.8bn) now exceeds domestic (£1.6bn)** ([Inside World Football](https://www.insideworldfootball.com/2026/03/02/premier-league-streaming-app-launch-singapore-starhub/)), and US, Nordic and MENA deals are up for renewal ([SportsPro](https://www.sportspro.com/analysis/finance-investment/premier-league-2026-27-season-tv-rights-sponsors-finances-valuations/)). **Inference:** any tool that makes the international feed more distinctive or easier to tailor by market supports the League's biggest growth lever. That is the strategic frame a pitch should use.

## The PL already owns chat, win probability and tracking stats, so avoid them

Microsoft became the PL's official cloud and AI partner in a five-year deal announced on 1 July 2025 ([Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2025/07/01/premier-league-and-microsoft-announce-five-year-strategic-partnership-to-personalize-the-fan-experience-with-ai-for-1-8-billion-people/)). The Copilot-powered Premier League Companion went live that day, drawing on 30+ seasons of stats, 300,000 articles and 9,000 videos. It launched with only three preset prompts, and TechRadar called it "useless – at least for now" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/artificial-intelligence/the-premier-league-companion-is-a-new-ai-powered-tool-for-soccer-fans-but-its-useless-at-least-for-now)). By November 2025 it handled natural-language questions, including live-match context such as **win probability after an early goal** ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/data-technologies/feature/Microsoft-helps-Premier-League-fuel-fan-experience-with-AI)). The FPL Companion launched for 2026/27 and **deliberately does not predict points or manage teams** ([Microsoft Source EMEA](https://news.microsoft.com/source/emea/features/fantasy-premier-league-companion-gives-managers-a-new-tool-for-success/)). The Microsoft roadmap also lists **AI narration, Spanish as the first added language, personalised clip compilations** and "real-time data overlays and post-match analysis" for the live match experience. None of these was confirmed live in the sources found ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/data-technologies/feature/Microsoft-helps-Premier-League-fuel-fan-experience-with-AI); [Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2025/07/01/premier-league-and-microsoft-announce-five-year-strategic-partnership-to-personalize-the-fan-experience-with-ai-for-1-8-billion-people/)).

## Rivals explain their numbers; PL fans get them late, unexplained and in four languages

The Bundesliga is the clearest benchmark. The DFL and AWS publish **16 named Match Facts**, each with its own definition and launch article, and use them on TV, in the app and in editorial ([Bundesliga.com](https://www.bundesliga.com/en/bundesliga/news/bundesliga-match-facts-aws-match-momentum-34052)). Match Momentum (Sept 2025) scores each team's threat from 0 to 10 over a five-minute window. It also generates automated story alerts such as "goal against the run of play" and "momentum flip" that feed graphics and tickers ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/media/bundesliga-match-fact-match-momentum-revealing-the-games-invisible-pulse)). The DFL's generative layer writes live tickers in several languages, in both a "professional journalist" style and a "casual" style, within about 7 seconds, with human review. The DFL reports **+20% app usage and +67% articles read** ([ZenML/Bundesliga](https://zenml.io/llmops-database/ai-powered-fan-engagement-and-content-personalization-for-global-football-audiences)). It also runs a "Data Story Finder" that surfaces stories for commentators and editors in real time ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/ai/generative-ai/customers/bundesliga/)).

Outside football, the pattern is predictive or explanatory overlays plus catch-up. Prime Vision circles likely blitzers and identifies defensive coverage before the snap. Its Rapid Recap compiles up to 13 highlights in under two minutes for viewers who join late ([About Amazon](https://aboutamazon.com/news/aws/prime-video-thursday-night-football-next-gen-stats-ai-features); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/amazons-ai-feature-for-nfl-broadcasts-predicts-which-defensive-players-will-blitz-the-quarterback/)). Among NFL fans with Prime who saw Prime Vision, **70% were positive** ([TV Tech](https://tvtechnology.com/news/amazons-prime-vision-nfl-feed-wins-over-fans-hub-says)). F1's Track Pulse ranks live stories with supporting data and pushes them to the production team ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/media/f1-revs-up-race-day-broadcasts-with-real-time-data-storytelling)). The NBA's AI-localised two-minute recaps in French, Portuguese and Spanish get about **75% average completion** (vendor-reported) ([WSC Sports](https://wsc-sports.com/blog/customer-spotlight/nba-launches-genai-technology-with-wsc-sports-to-automate-multilingual-content/)). Wimbledon's Match Chat answers live questions during matches ([IBM](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2025-06-17-the-all-england-lawn-tennis-club-and-ibm-launch-new-ai-features-for-real-time-wimbledon-fan-engagement)). Ligue 1 has a deal for AI-dubbed commentary ([TVBEurope](https://www.tvbeurope.com/artificial-intelligence/ready-for-kick-off-french-football-league-employs-ai-commentators)).

Fan evidence points to the same gaps. **Nearly 70% of fans want player metrics and live data, mostly before matches and in breaks rather than during play** ([Capgemini](https://www.capgemini.com/gb-en/news/press-releases/over-half-of-sports-fans-are-turning-to-ai-or-gen-ai-for-more-personalised-content/)). UK Gen Z fans check their phones almost ten times a game, mostly in natural breaks ([SVG](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2026/06/15/fifa-world-cup-2026-mid-half-water-breaks-create-new-opportunities-to-engage-gen-z-on-smartphones/)). Fans rank faster recaps (67%) and personalisation (65%) as top improvements, and 56% want AI-powered insights and commentary ([IBM](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2025-08-18-ibm-study-sports-fans-demand-more-dynamic-digital-content,-powered-by-ai)). Critics say broadcasters treat data as a niche add-on that arrives in articles hours later, and that unexplained single-match xG misleads viewers ([AnalyticsFC](https://analyticsfc.co.uk/blog/2023/04/05/is-football-media-making-the-most-of-football-data/); [StatsBomb](https://blogarchive.statsbomb.com/articles/soccer/xg-is-on-tv-now-what/)). Officiating communication is the sharpest pain point. In an FSA survey of nearly 8,000 fans, **fewer than 3% think VAR decisions arrive in reasonable time** ([LBC](https://www.lbc.co.uk/news/sport/premier-league-fans-var-fsa-study/)), and IFAB still bars live audio for VAR-reviewed incidents ([Qatar Tribune](https://www.qatar-tribune.com/article/249415/sports/premier-league-to-broadcast-on-field-referee-player-audio-conversations-live)). Fans also worry about trust: **57% fear AI could spread misinformation** ([Capgemini](https://www.capgemini.com/gb-en/news/press-releases/over-half-of-sports-fans-are-turning-to-ai-or-gen-ai-for-more-personalised-content/)). ESPN's AI recap launch drew criticism after a factual error ([Awful Announcing](https://awfulannouncing.com/tech/ai-moves-espn-tennis-channel-recaps-translation.html)).

Southeast Asia sharpens the case. The PL's relaunched site showed English, Spanish, Arabic and Thai, with **no Malay or Bahasa Indonesia** visible ([Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4337361/premier-league-launches-new-fan-facing-platforms-as-part-of-digital-transformation)). In Malaysia, Astro reports removing 580 Telegram piracy groups with 32.2 million subscribers ([Astro](https://corporate.astro.com.my/mediaroom-releases/premier-league-and-astro-warn-malaysian-football-fans-on-the-real-world-risks-of-piracy)). **Inference:** demand in the region is large and partly unserved by official channels. Kick-offs that land late at night locally push fans toward morning catch-up, so an explained "what you missed" brief in Malay fits a real gap. It also gives a Malaysia-based team an authentic angle that a London-built product lacks.

**Inference, consolidated gap list:** compared with rivals and fan demand, the PL ecosystem lacks (1) named, explainable live metrics with published definitions, (2) an automated story feed for the Studios gallery and partner broadcasters, (3) catch-up for fans who join late or watch the next morning, (4) a structured half-time and full-time narrative pushed to fans rather than pulled through chat, (5) persona-specific framing (casual vs analyst) beyond alerts, (6) Southeast Asian languages, and (7) a visible mechanism that makes AI claims verifiable. The evidence for 2, 4 and 5 is absence of evidence. The team should check the current PL app before stating these as fact on stage.

## Recommended concept: Match-Room Live, an explained-match engine from first whistle to full time

**Proposal.** Extend Match-Room from a half-time analysis and prediction site into "Match-Room Live". Every in-play insight it produces is timed, typed and evidence-linked, and those insights become the raw material for the half-time brief and the full-time story. The pitch line for judges is: *"Studios gets a story feed and machine-readable overlays; fans get the same evidence explained their way, in their language, at the moments they actually look at their phones."*

The pipeline maps directly onto the brief's five stages. **Ingest:** a Python generator simulates possession chains. Its zone-transition and action probabilities are estimated from open event data, with Wyscout's CC BY 4.0 dataset as the cleanest licence ([Nature Scientific Data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-019-0247-7)). Shot outcomes are sampled from the team's own xG model, and scripted storylines can be injected. The generator emits StatsBomb-shaped JSON in real time at configurable speed, through Azure Event Hubs or a websocket. Because the feed matches the StatsBomb format, socceraction (MIT) and kloppy (BSD-3) can parse it unchanged ([socceraction](https://github.com/ML-KULeuven/socceraction); [kloppy](https://github.com/PySport/kloppy)). The xT transition matrix serves both as the simulator kernel and as a metric, which saves build time ([Karun Singh](https://karun.in/blog/expected-threat.html)). **Interpret:** rolling-window computation of xG, xT per action, PPDA, field tilt, an Opta-style recency-weighted momentum, the control-vs-chaos composite and a simplified Poisson win probability. **Explain:** a deterministic insight engine fires typed objects such as `momentum_flip`, `pressure_spike`, `chaos_phase` and `against_the_run_of_play`, each carrying drivers (which players and actions moved the number) and a comparison baseline. **Render:** each insight becomes one overlay message with match clock, sequence number, template type, priority and time-to-live. A flattened key/value version maps to Vizrt-style `${key}` template placeholders ([Vizrt Datacenter](https://docs.vizrt.com/viz-trio-guide/4.1/Datacenter.html)). A transparent HTML overlay loads in OBS Browser Source for the demo ([OBS](https://obsproject.com/kb/browser-source)). **Personalise:** a profile (casual or analyst, favourite club, focus player, language, how many numbers to show) controls which insights surface and how they are worded. Azure OpenAI Structured Outputs return `headline`, `body` and `facts_used[]` ([Azure](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/blog/announcing-a-new-openai-feature-for-developers-on-azure/)). A code validator checks every number and name, in every language, before anything is shown.

The roll-up is what turns Match-Room's existing strength into the full-season story. **In play**, fans and the Studios console receive short cards, one sentence of 25 words or fewer per insight trigger rather than per event. This keeps latency within the roughly 7-second benchmark set by the DFL ticker ([ZenML/Bundesliga](https://zenml.io/llmops-database/ai-powered-fan-engagement-and-content-personalization-for-global-football-audiences)). **At half-time**, Match-Room assembles the half's insight log into a three-part brief: what happened (momentum and control timeline), why (the two or three highest-weighted drivers, each linked to its events), and what to watch (pressure trends and the explained projection, replacing today's bare prediction). Capgemini's finding that fans want data in breaks justifies the timing. **At full time**, the same log produces a match story with a "turning points" timeline and player-focus summaries. **Across the season**, full-time stories accumulate into team and player arcs: rolling control and chaos profiles, form, and which teams win "against the run of play". **Inference:** that covers the "whole season" part of the brief without a separate product.

For the demo, run one synthetic match at about 10x speed with an injected red card and comeback, so momentum flips arrive on cue. Show the same moment three ways: a broadcast lower-third, a casual card in Malay, and an analyst card in English with PPDA, xT drivers and the win-probability caveat that such numbers are long-run averages, not live odds ([KU Leuven DTAI](https://dtai.cs.kuleuven.be/sports/blog/a-bayesian-approach-to-in-game-win-probability)). Then open the "facts used" trail. A reasonable split for the team is that the data analyst owns the generator, the metrics, the insight rules and the validation checks (realism of distributions, fact consistency, CometKiwi quality scores for translations ([COMET](https://pypi.org/project/unbabel-comet/2.2.0))). The software developer owns streaming, the overlay contract, the HTML overlay and the Match-Room front end, and deployment on Azure Container Apps to match the hackathon's reference architecture. **The main engineering risk** is that socceraction expects a full match of events, so live use needs incremental conversion over a rolling window. The team should prototype this in the first days ([socceraction](https://github.com/ML-KULeuven/socceraction)). A second risk is Fabric capacity cost: Event Hubs plus a Python consumer is the cheaper Azure-native path ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/fabric/real-time-analytics/event-streams/overview)).

Several uncertainties remain. The hackathon's judging criteria and dataset schema were not public, so the team should check whether organisers supply their own synthetic feed and adapt the ingest stage to it. Whether the PL app now shows xG, written recaps or more languages was not verified for 2026/27. "Control vs chaos" has no published standard, so it must be presented as the team's own transparent composite with stated weights, not as an industry metric.

## Conclusion

The research changes where the opportunity lies. Studios was built for control and scale, and Microsoft's first products are retrieval and chat. Neither yet turns a live match into evidence-backed explanation pushed at the right moment. That makes "explainable" the hackathon's differentiator, not its decoration. Rival leagues show the formula: give each metric a name and a definition, generate stories for producers, and keep editors or validators in the loop. The fan data shows the timing: breaks, half-time and the morning after.

For Match-Room, the implication is to stop competing on prediction, which the PL already has, and start competing on accountability. Every number on screen should be traceable to the synthetic events that produced it, in the viewer's language and at their level of depth. A team in Kuala Lumpur pitching Malay-language, fact-checked half-time briefs to a London studio serving 189 markets brings a market perspective the official roadmap, which leads with Spanish, has not reached.
